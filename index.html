PrologueStory first

When everyone can build, the story becomes the differentiator.

Anyone can build. Tools are cheap, talent is everywhere, and that idea you had a few days ago can become a reality in minutes.

The same tools that made building easy, have made content easy too. The internet is filling with AI slop: content that is technically fine, but generic and monotone. The human judgment to find the true story, and tell it like a person, is rarer than it's ever been.

Narrative isn't a coat of paint you add once you've scaled. It's the thing that makes growth clear to customers, hires, investors. Get it wrong and there's a cost: prospects who ghost you, discovery calls that never move to the next stage, pitches that don't land at events.

Selling is hard, and selling without a story? It's impossible. That's where we start.

Ch. 05FAQ

Questions I get on the first call.

Why bring someone in for direction and not the writing?

Because sometimes the writing isn't the problem. Your team can put words on a page. What's missing is someone who decides what the story is, says no to the version that drifts, and keeps everyone telling it the same way. That's a senior job, and it's a few hours a month. You don't need a hire for it. You need someone to own it.

Couldn't I hire someone junior to take up the writing work?

You could, and the words would be fine. What you'd be missing is 15 years of learning what works. I know which LinkedIn posts start threads and which get scrolled past, and why. I know that a homepage headline gets one read before a buyer decides whether to keep going. I've written the customer story that a sales team never used, and figured out what it was missing. A junior writer will learn all of that too, eventually, on your budget and your timeline. Hiring me means you skip the years it takes to learn it.

Will the story stay consistent as we grow?

Only if it's owned collectively, and that's how I work. A story that lives with one person, me included, doesn't scale. So the messaging gets written down where everyone can find it, the words you use and the ones you don't are agreed rather than assumed, and the people who write for you, sales, marketing, leadership, work from the same page. My job over time is to help you keep that page current as the company changes, and to keep everyone telling the same story as it does.

Do you work with companies outside SaaS?

Yes. Telling a story is a skill whatever the product. Most of my recent work is B2B SaaS, but I've also worked in publishing, automotive and fintech, so I pick up an industry's language quickly and get to work.

Do you work with clients in the US, Europe or Australia?

Yes. I work across time zones. I've written for companies selling into all three markets and know how the buyers differ, down to the spelling.

How do you use AI in the work?

Often, but carefully. AI is good at drafts, structure and volume. It's bad at knowing what your story is and at sounding like a person. So the judgement stays with me: what the story is, what's true, what sounds like you. The tools do the parts that don't need a human, and I build them to your voice so the output doesn't drift into slop.

How do you price each engagement?

Against your scope, not a rate card. I'll quote once I understand what you're trying to move.

What's the minimum commitment you look for?

Three months to start: long enough to do real work, short enough to find out whether we're a fit. After that it's month to month, with a month's notice either side. You won't be asked to commit to work you haven't seen the shape of.
